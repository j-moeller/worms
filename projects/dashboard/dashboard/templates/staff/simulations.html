How a gameplan plays out, before a round uses it: its attempt runs on the orchestrator against a pretend clock (a wait(600) moves it by ten minutes; every other step takes the time its host job nominally takes) and is replayed here on the sandbox it describes. No worm runs in it: nothing is captured, and a file changes only when the gameplan writes it.

What a simulation shows

The sandbox as the gameplan describes it (VMs, networks, addresses, each VM's firewall rules) and its attempt as the conductor runs it: prepare (every VM gets its flag, the files written at reset), check, execute(), the rest of the run's time, every VM stopped, the drain, the teardown. A step that would fail in a round fails here too (resuming a running VM, a second deploy_worm(), ...).

What a real run keeps off the database stays off it here: files are numbered, in the order __init__ writes them (then the watch_file paths), and their paths, the gameplan's log lines and why a simulation failed are in the orchestrator's journal (journalctl -u orchestrator). A simulation is made once per version of the file and duration per team, then kept.
